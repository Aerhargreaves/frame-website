THE (RE)MADE COLLECTION

These pieces are a rebellion against obsolescence.

Made for MONC to celebrate LDF 2024. With a goal to challenge our definition of ‘waste’, we set out to create a one off line of ‘franken frames’ made from broken parts.

When an object is beyond repair we rarely consider its continued material potential. Each piece of the collection proves that re-circulation in our industry is entirely possible, yet we are encouraged to replace an eyewear frame every 2-3 years. Bio acetate can last 100+ years. Meaning at our current rate we’re using the material for under 5% of its potential lifespan.

This project explores the moment when something valuable becomes obsolete and asks the question, can we reinstate that value through reusing that object’s materials?

Within the four frames, I was able to use parts from 9 different pieces.
I find the narrative of this project so wonderful. Each design is only the way it is because of the one that has gone before, they’re characters and histories combine to form something brand new. For me this is what repair, recirculation, recycling is all about.

To me, the (RE)MADE collection is a call to action for our industry, it is searching for creative solutions to the issue of breakage and waste, but above that it is an invitation for all of us to rethink our relationship with our belongings. A pair of glasses is often a very precious thing to its wearer; they are an investment in a person’s eye health, style and identity. For around half our population it is an object we use daily and yet, when they break, they become waste.
