RE-FRAME

Reframe is an object driven research enquiry.

This is project trials a new design methodology.
Employing a scientific approach to the synthesis of research data in an attempt to create with more intention. The aim is to observe how a designer’s process impacts the efficacy of their output.

Corrective eyewear is reliant on the nose bridge to function correctly, the part of the face that changes most across race, gender and age. Their historically unchanged nature represents an industry that has become unobservant to the diversity of its user base.

The project culminates in three frames, unified in the removal of a nose bridge.
Each relies on just one input:

A: Historical alternatives
An act of critical fabulation. By re-creating historical alternatives to modern solutions, I was able to design a frame that does not oberserve the western standardisation of eyewear. Instead, the result explores how different contact points work better for specific phenotypic traits

B: Anthropometric data
A statistically optimised form, this understands the face through the lens of anthropological data. It’s form is driven by 2 of the most ‘stable’ dimensions across the human face. Through the analysis of an open-source data set*, 7 out of the 22 dimensions were Identified as parts of the face that change by less than 2% among people.

C: Co-designed solutions
Informed by recurring behavioural patterns seen within co-design participants. During the workshop participants were first asked to trace their facial features with craft wire and then create a form that could hold lenses without nasal anchorage. By observing their process & results I gained a more intimate and varied understanding of human features.
